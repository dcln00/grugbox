The Recording Academy announced the nominations for the 69th Annual Grammy Awards on November 16, 2026, ahead of the ceremony on February 7, 2027 at the Crypto.com Arena in Los Angeles. 

African artists took up significant space across both genre-specific and general categories, including four out of five spots in Best Global Music Album and two crossover nominations in R&B and Latin pop fields.

### The complete list of African nominees

Here is how African musicians appear across the 2027 Grammy categories:

#### Best African Music Performance
- Asake, "Gratitude"
- Wizkid featuring Asake, "Jogodo"
- Tems, "Big Daddy"
- Angélique Kidjo and Ayra Starr, "Aye Kan (Are You Coming Back?)"
- King Ayisoba featuring Rocky Dawuni, "No Tradition, No Africa"

#### Best Global Music Album
Four of the five entries in this field went to African artists:
- Davido, *Oriadé*
- Ayra Starr, *Starrgirl*
- Tems, *Love Is a Kingdom*
- Angélique Kidjo, *Hope!!*
The fifth slot belongs to Brazilian artist Anitta for *Equilibrvm*.

#### Best Global Music Performance
- Shakira and Burna Boy, "Dìá Dìá"
- Angélique Kidjo featuring Pharrell Williams and Quavo, "Bando [Orchestral]"
Other nominees in the category include Rosalía with Dougie F ("Porcelana"), Devan Ekambaram featuring Susha ("Varuvaaiy"), and Kerreke with Mireya and Alex E. Chávez ("Una Flor en Cada Estrella").

#### Best R&B Solo Performance
- Tems, "What You Need"

Across the wider ballot, British-Ghanaian singer RAYE led all artists with 11 total nominations, including nods for Album of the Year (*This Music May Contain Hope*), Record of the Year, and Song of the Year ("WHERE IS MY HUSBAND!").

### Ghana's entry: King Ayisoba and Rocky Dawuni

For the Ghanaian music industry, the major development is the inclusion of King Ayisoba and Rocky Dawuni in the Best African Music Performance category for their collaboration, "No Tradition, No Africa."

![Cover artwork for King Ayisoba featuring Rocky Dawuni - No Tradition, No Africa](/content/king-ayisoba-no-tradition-no-africa-cover.jpg)

<p class="text-xs text-center text-zinc-500 -mt-3 mb-6">Cover artwork for King Ayisoba featuring Rocky Dawuni, "No Tradition, No Africa"</p>

The song comes off King Ayisoba's seventh studio album, *Pure Confusion*, released in May 2026. Veteran Ghanaian producer Panji Anoff, whose Pidgen Music imprint helped shepherd hiplife in the late 1990s and documented Ayisoba's early street records in Accra, handled the production. The track pairs Ayisoba's two-stringed kologo and guttural vocal delivery with Dawuni's reggae cadence over an uptempo rhythm.

The nomination is Ayisoba's first. Born in Bongo in the Upper East Region, Ayisoba started as a traditional funeral musician and shepherd before moving south to Accra, playing bars and bus stations with his kologo slung over his back. His 2006 breakout, "I Want to See You, My Father," proved that northern traditional instrumentation could run national radio. This nomination brings the kologo tradition directly to the Recording Academy ballot.

For Rocky Dawuni, this marks his fifth Grammy nomination. Dawuni previously earned nods for *Branches of the Same Tree* (2016, Best Reggae Album), *Voice of Bunbon, Vol. 1* (2022, Best Global Music Album), "Never Bow Down" featuring Blvk H3ro (2023, Best Global Music Performance), and "Rise" (2025). He remains the most-nominated Ghanaian artist in Grammy history.

Ghanaian commentary around the Grammys often revolves around the commercial gap between Accra and Lagos, with recurrent local debates about why mainstream Ghanaian hiplife, drill, or afropop acts struggle to secure votes in Los Angeles. "No Tradition, No Africa" bypassed that formula entirely. It earned its spot through Panji Anoff's independent studio in Accra and an indigenous instrument from the savannah, keeping the lyric focused on cultural survival.
