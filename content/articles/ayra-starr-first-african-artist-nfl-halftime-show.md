On Sunday, October 11, 2026, Nigerian singer-songwriter **Ayra Starr** etched her name into the history books, becoming the first African artist to headline an **NFL Halftime Show**. 

Taking the stage at **Tottenham Hotspur Stadium** in London during the NFL International Games matchup between the **Philadelphia Eagles** and the **Jacksonville Jaguars**, the 24-year-old Afrobeats star commanded a crowd of over 60,000 in-stadium fans and millions watching globally via live television broadcast.

### A Defining Moment for Afrobeats on the World Stage

Presented by American Express, the halftime spectacle was a centerpiece of the NFL’s expanding global presence. For Starr, it represented another monumental breakthrough in a career that has continually shattered glass ceilings.

Backed by a full ensemble of dancers dressed in sleek black streetwear accented with vibrant royal-purple sashes, the Mavin Records superstar brought the kinetic rhythm and infectious energy of contemporary Lagos straight to North London. Dressed in a corseted lace bodice and knee-high leather boots, she delivered an electrifying, choreography-heavy set that kept the stadium roaring from kickoff to the final whistle.

### Hits, Harmonies, and High Voltage

Starr’s halftime setlist offered a masterclass in dynamic pop-Afrobeats performance. She powered through a run of crowd favorites and fan anthems, including her diamond-certified global smash **"Rush,"** the seductive groove of **"Hot Body,"** and the bouncy rhythms of **"Heaven Baby,"** before dipping into tracks from her latest body of work, *Starrgirl*. 

Every transition showcased her vocal control and stage magnetism, proving that the African sound is not just participating in global sports entertainment—it is headlining it.

### Pitch-Side Playfulness to Global Primetime

Before the lights flared and the pyrotechnics signaled the start of her performance, Starr took to the stadium turf for a playful pre-game portrait shoot. Sporting a mod black-and-white horizontal-striped mini dress with pointed kitten heels and an official Wilson NFL football at her feet, she embraced the grandeur of the NFL stage with the effortless swagger that fans have come to revere.

Over the last few years, African music has consistently claimed some of global sports' grandest platforms—from Burna Boy, Rema, and Tems headlining the NBA All-Star Weekend to Burna Boy performing at the UEFA Champions League Final. Yet, stepping onto an NFL halftime stage marks a milestone all its own, unlocking one of the largest and most coveted commercial stages in American sports culture.

For Ayra Starr, this historic afternoon in London was more than just a victory lap—it was a resounding statement: the "Celestial Being" is only just getting started.
