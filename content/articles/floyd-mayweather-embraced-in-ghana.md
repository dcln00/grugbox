Floyd Mayweather Jr. touched down in Accra on November 18, 2026, for a multi-day working tour, and the reception across the capital was immediate. Crowds trailed his convoy from Kotoka International Airport straight into the coastal enclave of Bukom, the historic home of Ghanaian boxing.

Bukom has produced more world champions per square mile than almost anywhere else on the continent, from D.K. Poison to Azumah Nelson and Ike Quartey. When Mayweather arrived at the Bukom Boxing Arena, thousands of residents, aspiring young fighters, and neighborhood regulars packed the stands and overflowed onto the surrounding streets.

The Ghana Tourism Authority formally made Mayweather a Ghana Tourism Ambassador during the arena stop, presenting him with a traditional handwoven smock (fugu). Standing alongside him were Ghanaian boxing royalty: three-time world champion Azumah Nelson and former WBA welterweight champion Ike "Bazooka" Quartey. Local fighter and entertainer Bukom Banku stepped up to the ring apron for a series of playful pad drills and dance steps, drawing huge cheers from the arena.

Mayweather also made several official calls across the city, accompanied by Ghanaian entrepreneur Nana Kwame Bediako and Sharaf Mahama. At the Ga Mantse palace, King Tackie Teiko Tsuru II welcomed him with traditional Ga blessings, where Mayweather tried out a few Ga phrases with the elders. Later in the week, he met with President John Dramani Mahama to talk about sports development, youth training facilities, and the logistics of bringing sanctioned international bouts to Accra.

During his meetings with youth boxers in Jamestown, Mayweather pledged resources to equip local gyms with modern gear and discussed plans for a dedicated training program to support upcoming talent. For a neighborhood where gyms run on bare floors, heavy bags, and raw determination, having the undefeated champion walk through the door and commit practical backing gave the boxing community plenty to talk about.
