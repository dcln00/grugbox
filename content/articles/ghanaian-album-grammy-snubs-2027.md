The 2027 Grammy nominations arrived this morning, and like clockwork, the annual debate over who got snubbed has taken over the timeline. This year, the eligibility window brought some of the most cohesive, ambitious full-length projects out of Ghana in years. I had high hopes that after years of conversation around African music at the Recording Academy, we would finally see Ghanaian albums recognized in the global fields.

Instead, the final ballot delivered a familiar story. In Best Global Music Album, Nigerian acts took four of the five slots: Davido's *Oriadé*, Ayra Starr's *Starrgirl*, Tems's *Love Is a Kingdom*, and Angélique Kidjo's *Hope!!* (with Brazilian pop star Anitta filling the fifth spot). While King Ayisoba and Rocky Dawuni secured a well-deserved nod in Best African Music Performance for "No Tradition, No Africa", not a single Ghanaian LP made it into the album categories.

![African Artists at the 2027 Grammy Nominations](/content/2027-grammy-nominations-african-artists.jpg)

### Why Nigeria dominates and Ghana gets left behind

Every November, Ghanaian Twitter asks the same frustrated question: why does Nigeria sweep the African categories while Ghana struggles to get a look?

It is easy to blame musical quality, but that explanation does not hold up when you actually listen to the records released this year. The disparity comes down to machinery, membership, and money:

1. **Major label infrastructure:** Lagos is the West African headquarters for Sony Music, Universal Music Group, and Warner Music. Nigerian superstars have international A&R teams, dedicated PR agencies in London and New York, and major-label marketing budgets pushing their projects directly to American taste-makers. Ghanaian artists, by contrast, are almost entirely self-funded or signed to local boutique labels operating without international distribution muscle.
2. **Recording Academy voting members:** A Grammy nomination is not decided by public streaming numbers. It is voted on by peers in the Recording Academy. Over the last five years, Nigeria has successfully onboarded dozens of producers, executives, artists, and engineers into Academy voting membership. Ghana still has only a tiny handful of voting members (including Rocky Dawuni and Panji Anoff). When first-round ballots go out, Nigerian releases already have a coordinated voting constituency. Ghanaian submissions enter the room virtually alone.
3. **The "For Your Consideration" machine:** A serious Grammy campaign costs tens of thousands of dollars in FYC print ads, digital campaigns on Billboard and Variety, and private listening dinners with Los Angeles academy members. Nigerian labels finance these campaigns as routine business investments. Ghanaian artists, paying out of pocket in a volatile cedi economy, simply cannot compete with that spending.

The talent in Ghana is operating at an all-time peak, but without voting power and campaign infrastructure, great albums keep getting treated like they do not exist.

Here are the five Ghanaian album snubs that deserved a spot on the 2027 Grammy ballot.

---

### Snub 1: AratheJay: *The Odyssey (Live)*

![AratheJay - The Odyssey (Live)](/content/arathejay-the-odyssey-live-cover.jpg)

This album is a masterclass in musicianship. Released in May 2026, *The Odyssey (Live)* recorded AratheJay alongside the Musical Lunatics Band across 17 live tracks, tossing out studio auto-tune in favor of raw brass, live percussion, and rich choral arrangements. Songs like "Sankofa (Live)" and "Jesus (Live)" carry a spiritual weight that completely silences a room. When we reviewed it on Grugbox, it earned a 9.2 score, the highest of the entire year. 

To have an authentic, live African spiritual recording of this caliber completely ignored by the Global Music committee is heartbreaking.

- **What I would have nominated it for:** Best Global Music Album

---

### Snub 2: Black Sherif: *SUN SHERIF*

![Black Sherif - SUN SHERIF](/content/black-sherif-sun-sherif-cover.jpg)

Everyone knows Black Sherif can deliver heavy street grief and high-octane drill, but on *SUN SHERIF*, he did something far more daring: he let the light in. The 6-track project leaned into warm highlife guitar lines, acoustic textures, and vocal vulnerability. "Forever", his duet with AratheJay, is easily one of the most gorgeous vocal performances out of West Africa this year, showing a young artist widening his sonic palette with total confidence.

Leaving Blacko off the ballot after such a creative leap feels like a major missed opportunity for the Academy to celebrate versatility.

- **What I would have nominated it for:** Best Global Music Album (or Best African Music Performance for "Forever")

---

### Snub 3: Kwesi Arthur: *Redemption Valley*

![Kwesi Arthur - Redemption Valley](/content/kwesi-arthur-redemption-valley-cover.jpg)

After years of label disputes, quiet frustrations, and relocation, Kwesi Arthur returned on February 6, 2026, with an album that discarded radio formulas entirely. *Redemption Valley* fused 90s indie and grunge chords with unvarnished Tema street lyricism. Tracks like "Broken Pieces" and "Yawa (Hosanna)" gave listeners a painfully honest look at contractual heartbreak and creative survival. 

It is one of the bravest independent rap records out of Africa this decade, and it proved that Kwesi never needed commercial compromises to make monumental art.

- **What I would have nominated it for:** Best Global Music Album or Best Alternative Music Album

---

### Snub 4: Kweku Smoke: *No Days Off*

![Kweku Smoke - No Days Off](/content/kweku-smoke-no-days-off-cover.jpg)

Nobody in Ghanaian hip-hop outworks Kweku Smoke. Dropping his seventh full-length project on September 4, 2026, *No Days Off* delivered 13 tracks of razor-sharp cadence, soulful sample flips, and hard-earned street wisdom. With standout features from Lasmid and Kwesi Arthur, Smoke demonstrated how independent African hip-hop can stand shoulder-to-shoulder with any international rap record.

If the Grammys want to honor contemporary African rap beyond the usual pop singles, this was the project to pick.

- **What I would have nominated it for:** Best Melodic Rap Performance or Best Global Music Performance (for "Faith" featuring Lasmid)

---

### Snub 5: O'Kenneth: *CAME OF AGE*

![O'Kenneth - CAME OF AGE](/content/okenneth-came-of-age-cover.jpg)

When Kumasi drill first exploded, skeptics claimed the sound was too aggressive to sustain a full artistic journey. On *CAME OF AGE*, released on May 1, 2026, O'Kenneth silenced those doubts for good. Over 18 tracks, he slowed down the tempos, brought in live guitars, and reflected on lost friends and the disorientation of rapid fame. Supported by his Asakaa brothers Kwaku DMC and Chicogod, Kenneth matured the genre into reflective, melancholic storytelling.

It stands as an essential document of young Ghanaian youth culture that deserved international recognition.

- **What I would have nominated it for:** Best Global Music Album
