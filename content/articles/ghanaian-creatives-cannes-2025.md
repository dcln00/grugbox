The Cannes Film Festival has wrapped up, and as always it was a busy stretch of cinema, red carpet moments, and industry buzz. But beyond the headlines, there is news closer to home too. Ghanaian creatives were on the ground at Cannes this year, and they were there to work.

Cannes is known for film, but it is also a hub for the wider creative industry, especially with the Cannes Lions Festival running alongside. This year the talk was all about creator-led content, and Ghana was part of it.

Let's shout out some of the Ghanaian trailblazers who took the stages and rubbed shoulders with global giants:

Our very own culinary queen, Abena Sintim-Aboagye, popularly known as Chef Abby, wasn't just there to serve looks; she was serving up cultural ambassadorship. She was spotted with TikTok's global CEO, a pairing that showed the reach of African creators. And she linked up with viral food critic Keith Lee.

Enil Art, also known as Israel Derrick Apeti from Woof Studios, is the digital storytelling and motion graphics wizard who brought his A-game to the Cannes circuit. He painted a canvas of the Fontaine de la Place du Général de Gaulle and filmed the process on Meta Smart Glasses for real-time content. He also surprised everyone with a live mini-portrait of Issa Rae during a creator session.

We also had Mister Meister and Natasha Mann-Dedey, co-founders of Jambo Spaces, representing Ghana at the event. Jambo Spaces blends creative co-working with community building, and the pair were showing how our creatives are building the foundation for future generations of artists and storytellers.

Finally, Adele and Maame Yaa Adubea, winners of the Young Lions Africa Digital Competition. They clinched the competition, which earned them a direct ticket to Cannes and put them in front of top global agencies and brands. It was an inspiring moment for Ghana's next wave of digital talent.

So, what does all this mean for the Ghanaian creative industry?

This presence at Cannes is about more than a few individuals shining. It matters for our entire creative sector. Here's why:

- Global recognition and visibility: Our creatives on the global stage mean Ghana is being seen, heard, and recognised for its talent and innovation. That opens doors to collaborations, investment, and more international opportunities.
- Inspiration for the next generation: When young Ghanaian creatives see their own making it big on platforms like Cannes, it fuels their dreams and pushes them to aim higher. It sends a clear message to the next wave of Ghanaian talent.
- Shifting the narrative: For too long, African stories and creativity have been under-represented or misrepresented. Our presence at Cannes helps reclaim that narrative, showing the world the richness, depth, and diversity of Ghanaian and African culture.
- A catalyst for growth: This global exposure can attract more investment into our creative industries. This exposure can pull more investment into our creative industries, and it makes the case to local and international stakeholders that music, film, fashion and digital content are worth backing.
- Networking and learning: Beyond the spotlight, Cannes is a chance for our creatives to learn from the best, connect with industry leaders, and bring back insights and trends to further develop our local scene.

The world is finally catching on. When it comes to creativity, Africa isn't an "emerging market", it's the "now market". Our Ghanaian creatives are shaping narratives, building platforms, and commanding global stages.

This is an exciting time for the Ghanaian creative industry, and with every step our talents take on the international scene, we're building a stronger and more recognised creative economy right here at home. And to all creatives alike, let's keep the fire burning.
