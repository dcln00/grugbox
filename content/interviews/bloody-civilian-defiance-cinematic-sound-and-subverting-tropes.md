Emoseh Khamofu, performing under the moniker **Bloody Civilian**, is arguably the most radical young sonic disruptor to emerge from Northern Nigeria in recent memory. A self-taught producer, multi-instrumentalist, singer, and visual storyteller, she burst onto the international radar with her inclusion on Marvel's *Black Panther: Wakanda Forever* soundtrack, followed by her critically acclaimed EP *Anger Management*.

Her work is fierce, satirical, cinematic, and profoundly intelligent—a collision of brass fanfares, electronic synths, and blistering socio-political commentary. Grugbox sat down with the Kaduna-born visionary to discuss weaponizing satire, writing for cinema, and debunking industry expectations.

---

### Making Music with Teeth

**Grugbox: Your stage name—Bloody Civilian—stems from a notorious phrase used by military personnel in Nigeria. What does reclaiming that language mean to your art?**

**Bloody Civilian:** It’s about power dynamics. In Nigeria, 'bloody civilian' is what soldiers yell to remind ordinary citizens that they are expendable, unprotected, second-class. By taking that insult and wearing it as my banner, I flipped the script. It’s a declaration that the ordinary citizen has a voice, has fury, and has agency. My music doesn't mince words because our reality doesn't mince consequences.

**Grugbox: You produce and engineer nearly all your own music. Why was claiming absolute control over the production board so essential for you?**

**Bloody Civilian:** Because when I was starting out, every male producer I approached tried to reduce me to a generic R&B songbird. They wanted pretty melodies and soft harmonies. But in my head, I heard distorted kick drums, violent brass stabs, and erratic BPM shifts. Nobody could hear what was inside my head except me. So I locked myself in a room, spent thousands of hours learning Logic and Ableton, and taught myself to build every frequency from scratch.

---

### Cinema and the Future of African Storytelling

**Grugbox: Your songs have an unmistakably narrative, almost cinematic scope. How do you approach composing for screen versus creating standalone records?**

**Bloody Civilian:** To me, there is zero distinction between a record and a film score. When I write a song, I always visualize a scene first: What is the lighting? Who is running? What is burning in the background? African cinema—especially the new wave of indie cinema emerging from Lagos, Accra, and Nairobi—needs soundtracks that aren't just background noise, but psychological catalysts. I want my scores to make the audience's skin prickle.

**Grugbox: What is the biggest lesson you have learned about staying sane in this industry?**

**Bloody Civilian:** Never measure your worth by the approval of gatekeepers who couldn't write a four-bar melody if their lives depended on it. Trust your intuition, build a loyal community of real listeners, and never create from a place of fear.
