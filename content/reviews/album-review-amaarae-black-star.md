_THE ANGEL YOU DON'T KNOW_ arrived in 2020, _Fountain Baby_ in 2023, and between them Amaarae changed what international listeners expected from African pop. The template they had in mind was mid-tempo Afrobeats. She answered with punk guitars, Y2K Japanese pop, whisper-funk, and songwriting that ignored the brief entirely.

_Black Star_, released on August 8, 2025, aims at a bigger canvas still. The title refers to Ghana's national emblem and to Marcus Garvey's shipping line, and the album sets out to push African pop into a hyper-stylised, afro-futuristic register. Across 13 tracks it very nearly does. What holds it back is not a shortage of ideas. It is the number of them.

## The trouble with maximalism

_Fountain Baby_ worked because every wild pivot was anchored to a pop song you could hum on the way home. On _Black Star_ that balance slips.

Amaarae throws in everything: four-on-the-floor garage breaks, metallic industrial distortion, Brazilian funk, choral gospel. When an arrangement leaves room for her melodies, the result is euphoric. Too often it does not. The whisper is her best instrument, and here it spends much of the record competing with production loud enough to bury the hook.

## The tracklist

### 1. Stuck Up & 2. Starkilla (feat. Bree Runway, Starkillers)

_Stuck Up_ opens on icy, menacing bass, holding a haughty runway pose before crashing into _Starkilla_. British-Ghanaian rapper **Bree Runway** and **Starkillers** turn the second half into four-on-the-floor electro-punk, trading verses with supervillain relish. It is the sharpest jolt on the record.

### 3. ms60 (feat. Naomi Campbell) & 4. Kiss Me Thru The Phone Pt 2 (feat. PinkPantheress)

_ms60_ hands the mic to **Naomi Campbell** for a spoken-word passage built entirely around runway drama. It works as a setup for the album's best song. _Kiss Me Thru The Phone Pt 2_ brings in **PinkPantheress** for a buoyant 2-step garage track that interpolates Soulja Boy. Their airy vocals trade lines over skittering breakbeats, and the whole thing sounds effortless in a way nothing else here quite manages.

### 5. B2B, 6. She Is My Drug, & 7. Girlie-Pop!

The midsection goes for chaotic club hedonism. _Girlie-Pop!_ pulls it off: sugary, bouncy, genuinely joyful. _B2B_ and _She Is My Drug_ do not. Between the abrasive tempo switches and the erratic vocal processing, both sound closer to unfinished runway demos than finished songs.

### 8. S.M.O.

_S.M.O._ tries to bridge the album's two halves with hypnotic percussion and low-end hums. It drifts for four minutes without finding a hook, and ends up functioning as transitional glue.

![Black Star Official Tracklist Part 2](/content/amaarae-black-star-tracklist-02.jpg)

### 9. Fineshyt & 10. Dove Cameron

The back half restores some breathing room. _Fineshyt_ rides a sultry, liquid guitar groove and finally lets Amaarae's sensuality carry a song on its own. _Dove Cameron_ goes hazy and psychedelic, with a vulnerability that lands because it is not competing with anything.

### 11. Dream Scenario (feat. Charlie Wilson) & 12. 100DRUM (feat. Zacari)

**Charlie Wilson** is the unexpected coup here. His gospel tenor on _Dream Scenario_ sets up a dialogue across generations that the album does not really earn anywhere else. _100DRUM_, with TDE's **Zacari**, drifts into psychedelic trap and is pleasant enough, though its languid pace sits oddly this late in the running order.

### 13. FREE THE YOUTH

The album closes on _FREE THE YOUTH_, a tribute to the Accra creative collective that reshaped Ghanaian youth culture. Celebratory horns, communal chants, live percussion. It is the one moment where the scale of the production matches the scale of the idea.

## The verdict

_Black Star_ is a bold record from one of the most inventive minds in African music. At 7/10, its highs, particularly _Kiss Me Thru The Phone Pt 2_ and _FREE THE YOUTH_, belong alongside the best of her catalogue.

The restless maximalism and the erratic sequencing keep it short of _Fountain Baby_, though. It is a thrilling and occasionally exhausting listen, and it suggests that the next Amaarae record might be a better one for a little restraint.
