Pre-recorded backing tracks, auto-tune and synchronized lip-syncing are standard on festival stages. Genuine live musicianship has become the exception in African popular music.

AratheJay is one of the exceptions. **_The Odyssey (Live)_** was captured during his _Live Session 002_ and released on May 25, 2026. The Ashaiman-born singer-songwriter and producer does something very few of his peers attempt: he takes his debut album and rebuilds it from the ground up with a live band, **The Musical Lunatics Band**.

The result is not a stopgap live recording. It sets a new benchmark for modern African live albums.

## The telepathy of the Lunatics

What holds _The Odyssey (Live)_ together is the palpable understanding between AratheJay and his backing band. There are no synthetic shortcuts here. The snare hits are acoustic and textured, the basslines are played rather than programmed, and the horns punch with the attack of a church band. The electric guitars use classic Ghanaian palmwine and highlife phrasing.

AratheJay himself is in strong voice. Free of the studio booth, his vocals stretch, improvise, crack with real feeling and reach into effortless falsetto. He holds the stage like a gospel preacher, with the looseness of a jazz frontman.

## The tracklist

![The Odyssey Official Tracklist Artwork](/content/arathejay-the-odyssey-live-tracklist.jpg)

### 1. Zion (Live) & 2. Cover Me (Live)

The set opens with _Zion (Live)_. The horn section slowly breathes warmth into the room before breaking into a jubilant, polyrhythmic groove. AratheJay's vocal delivery is impassioned, and the song turns into an anthem of divine refuge. It flows straight into _Cover Me (Live)_, where the band strips back to a delicate bass-and-guitar dialogue and lets his raw acoustic voice carry the listener.

### 3. Talisman (Live) & 4. Fire (Live)

_Talisman_ brings in intricate talking drums and syncopated percussion. It builds into the intensity of _Fire (Live)_, where the electric guitar solos trade fiery licks with AratheJay's escalating vocal runs. The energy never dips, and he sounds like an artist completely consumed by the performance.

### 5. Ahuofe (Live)

_Ahuofe_, Akan for _Beauty_, is slowed down to a tender, midnight crawl. Over warm Rhodes chords and a muted trumpet solo that evokes 1960s Accra nightlife, AratheJay serenades his muse with an intimacy that borders on voyeuristic. It is soulful, and the finest moment here.

### 6. Ground Zero (Live) & 7. Ara No Dey Sleep (Live)

On _Ground Zero_ AratheJay revisits where he started, and the band builds a swelling, cinematic tension that erupts into a celebratory crescendo. _Ara No Dey Sleep_ follows with rapid tempo switches. The band's tightness shows as they stop on a dime and sprint through complex rhythmic intervals.

### 8. Put Am On God (Live) & 9. Alhaji Popping (Live)

_Put Am On God (Live)_ is the spiritual climax of the set, a full-blown Pentecostal praise session that leaves the musicians visibly moved. _Alhaji Popping_ caps things off with a playful, high-spirited run through the song, and AratheJay leaves the stage to roaring applause.

## The verdict

**_The Odyssey (Live)_** is the strongest argument in years for Ghanaian music played on organic instruments, in front of a live audience. At 9.2/10, it makes its case through the band, a shared spiritual charge and disciplined playing.

AratheJay and The Musical Lunatics Band have done more than perform these songs. They have made a document of an album that will be studied for years to come.
