Tema has long been the benchmark for Ghanaian musical ingenuity. Sarkodie's clinical rap cadences, R2Bees' highlife luxury and Kwesi Arthur's grounded street melodies all came out of the same coastal city, and its reputation rests on voices that changed the national sound.

**Kweku Pee** (Derrick Kweku Phidel Aditeye) is the latest to try. He has steadily built a following on the underground circuit, and with **_Kp Sound_**, released on September 24, 2026 via Alkians Entertainment World, he tries to turn that early momentum into a definitive 7-track debut statement.

## The pulse and the pitfalls

Kweku Pee's vocal stamina is real. He does not ease into songs; he charges at them like an artist trying to prove himself within ten seconds. On _Kp Sound_ that ambition drives buoyant Afrobeats grooves, punchy log drums and bright brass horns built for immediate street-level impact.

Stamina alone does not fix a lack of original writing. Across much of the EP, Kweku Pee defaults to familiar genre blueprints instead of finding a style of his own. His cadences echo contemporary street-pop forebears without adding fresh emotional nuance. Where Tema's icons had unmistakable points of view, _Kp Sound_ too often sounds like a pastiche of what works on TikTok and roadside speakers.

## The tracklist

### 1. 11

Opening cut _11_ sets his tempo right away. Galloping Afro-house percussion and repetitive chants make it work as an adrenaline pump, though the lyrics vanish from memory the moment the beat stops.

### 2. Mea (feat. Yaw Tog)

The clear standout. Teaming up with Asakaa pioneer **Yaw Tog**, Kweku Pee gets his best chorus on the record, and Tog brings his trademark gravel-toned punchlines. It is an effective meeting of Tema swagger and Kumerican drill bounce, and it shows what Pee can do with a strong sparring partner.

### 3. Agbada (feat. BigMoon) & 4. Command 2.0 (feat. Glorious Voices & Flayma)

_Agbada_ is standard celebratory fare, and it leans on **BigMoon** to flesh out an upbeat flex anthem. _Command 2.0_ pivots into church-born reverence. With **Glorious Voices** and **Flayma** on choral backing, Kweku Pee sounds genuinely grateful, and it is a rare flash of emotional sincerity on the EP.

### 5. Osanle & 6. Sexual Healing

The momentum stumbles in the romantic midsection. _Osanle_ and _Sexual Healing_ try to cast Kweku Pee as a suave Afro-R&B loverboy, but the writing leans on tired tropes and the posturing does not convince. Neither track lands as seductive, and in a crowded Afropop market they do not stand out.

### 7. Woara

Closing track _Woara_ lifts the energy again with brisk highlife guitars and uptempo percussion. While it is catchy and well produced, it stays on safe, predictable ground, and the record ends on a pleasant but familiar groove.

## The verdict

_Kp Sound_ is a respectable if unremarkable first record. Kweku Pee has raw charisma, vocal power and an ear for punchy production, but safe choices and interchangeable songwriting hold the project back.

At 6/10 it works as a portfolio piece that proves his vocal chops. To inherit Tema's mantle, though, Kweku Pee will need to step out of his influences' shadow and find a pen of his own.
