When Moliy's breathy cadence floated across the 2021 remix of _Sad Girlz Luv Money_ alongside Amaarae and Kali Uchis, listeners got an early look at West Africa's new-school alté scene. She brought a light touch, crystalline melodies and a celebration of feminine autonomy.

Turning that charisma into a durable, full-length musical statement is harder than it looks. On her official debut mixtape, **_BADDIES ♥ MOLIY_** (released on July 31, 2026), the Ghanaian-American singer-songwriter leans on high-fashion internet branding, late-night VIP tropes and hyper-kinetic club tempos.

The packaging is polished to a mirror shine. The music underneath struggles to hold up across 12 tracks, and style ends up outpacing substance again and again.

## The illusion of the VIP section

The central conflict of _BADDIES ♥ MOLIY_ is its insistence on keeping up appearances at all costs. The mixtape builds an exclusive, bottle-service universe where vulnerability counts as a defect and emotional detachment is the currency.

That aloof posture works for a three-minute viral banger. Across a whole project it wears thin. Moliy delivers almost every line in the same whisper-cadence, rarely modulating her vocal dynamics or letting the listener into the interior life behind the designer sunglasses. The production bounces constantly between Caribbean dembow and brisk Afro-house, and the mixtape rarely pauses to breathe. There is little room left for melody or memory to take root.

## The tracklist

### 1. TOMETO & 2. JETSKI (FRIKI) (feat. Yailin La Más Viral & Theodora)

The mixtape opens with _TOMETO_, a breezy, sing-song flex that feels more like an extended social media teaser than an album opener. It bleeds directly into _JETSKI (FRIKI)_, an uptempo Latin-Caribbean dembow workout featuring **Yailin La Más Viral** and **Theodora**. The beat knocks with club authority. Moliy's vocals sound strangely detached, swallowed by the aggressive production.

### 3. POPSTAR XOXO & 4. BUKSHOT

_POPSTAR XOXO_ attempts a glossy Y2K pop crossover, but the hook leans on repetitive refrains that grow grating after a couple of spins. The track cuts off abruptly to make way for _BUKSHOT_, where Moliy adopts Jamaican patois inflections over distorted 808s. The posturing feels theatrical rather than lived-in, all sonic cosplay and no conviction.

### 5. PARTYGYAL (feat. bees & honey) & 6. BODY GO (feat. Tyla)

The tape finds temporary salvation on track six. _PARTYGYAL_ dissolves into generic sound-system filler. _BODY GO_ is the one that works. Teaming up with South African star **Tyla**, the pair find real chemistry over a sultry bed of Amapiano log drums and Afro-R&B keys. It is the one moment where Moliy's sensual vocal approach is fully in command of its surroundings.

### 7. 911 (CALL THE LAW) & 8. BOUNCE

_911 (CALL THE LAW)_ pumps urgent Afro-house tempo into the room and works reasonably well as a dancefloor detonator. _BOUNCE_ follows immediately with nearly identical rhythmic phrasing. It is the mixtape's pacing problem in miniature: tracks blur into one another without distinct melodic architecture.

### 9. ACCRA GIRLS (feat. Sarkodie & Mellissa) & 10. PRETTY MAMI (feat. MAVO)

_ACCRA GIRLS_ is the project's strongest cultural anchor. Ghanaian rap veteran **Sarkodie** brings vintage charisma, and rising alté singer **Mellissa** complements Moliy's hook on an affectionate ode to Accra's cosmopolitan women. It is a highlight, undercut immediately by the forgettable, hurried filler of _PRETTY MAMI_.

### 11. BACKIE & 12. SHAKE IT TO THE MAX (REMIX)

The final stretch exposes the tape's structural problems. _BACKIE_ clocks in as a repetitive, skeletal sketch that ends before it develops into a full song. The mixtape closes with the all-star remix of _SHAKE IT TO THE MAX_, recruiting dancehall heavyweights **Skillibeng** and **Shenseea**. Their verses bring real energy, but the track still feels like an outsourced rescue job.

## The verdict

**_BADDIES ♥ MOLIY_** has real high points: _BODY GO_ and _ACCRA GIRLS_ prove Moliy has the star power to share a stage with anyone.

At 5.8/10, though, the mixtape is held back by rushed song structures and repetitive themes, and by a preference for style over musical substance. It works as background fuel for late-night pre-drinks. As a statement from one of West Africa's brightest lights, it leaves the listener wanting more.
