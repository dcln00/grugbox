Black Sherif is, above all else, an exceptional writer.

The more you sit with his discography, album after album and EP after EP, the more obvious it becomes how deeply rooted his writing is in lived experience. Much of Kwaku Killa's catalogue is built on friction: street poverty, early betrayal, and songs about what survival actually costs.

With **_SUN SHERIF_**, released on September 4, 2026, the climate visibly softens. The clouds part, the trench coats come off, and Black Sherif steps into golden-hour daylight.

## The palate cleanser

Artists cannot stay in crisis mode without burning out, and _SUN SHERIF_ is Blacko giving himself permission to exhale. Following the weight of _The Villain I Never Was_ and _Iron Boy_, this six-track EP is a deliberate, sunny palate cleanser.

Here the Konongo native explores a lighter, more carefree romantic persona. Warm highlife guitar licks, easy percussion and relaxed vocals keep the mood unburdened. That lightness also lowers the stakes. Without the urgency that drives his best work, some of these songs settle into standard Afropop territory rather than pushing past it.

## The tracklist

### 1. Expresso

_Expresso_ opens the EP on an easy, mid-tempo groove. Blacko sounds relaxed and self-assured, taking a rare moment to savor the view from the top. It sets up the EP's breezy mood, though it is more of a warm-up than an opener you will return to.

### 2. Run Around

The standout of the EP. _Run Around_ is golden-hour nostalgia: the knot-in-the-chest feeling of falling hard for someone while suspecting they are playing games with you. The vintage highlife swing and buoyant bassline make it the one you replay.

### 3. Forever (feat. AratheJay)

A celebration of brotherhood, longevity and shared roots. Teaming up with fellow lyricist **AratheJay**, _Forever_ glides. Their complementary cadences lock into a warm sunset groove, like a toast between peers who survived the same storm.

### 4. Jolie

_Jolie_ finds Blacko at his most melodically tender, serenading his muse with an unarmored vocal take. It is sweet and endearing, and it shows his vocal range, though the romantic tropes are simpler than the layered storytelling fans expect from him.

### 5. Frontline

If you wondered whether Kwaku Killa had retired his battle instincts, _Frontline_ interrupts the sweet melodies with a flash of bite. Confident and sharp-edged, it adds tension to the tracklist. The competitive hunger is still there.

### 6. Love Again (feat. Full Blown)

Teaming up with Trinidadian duo **Full Blown**, the EP closes on a buoyant Caribbean note. _Love Again_ is about taking another chance on romance after heartbreak. It is a pleasant, optimistic ending, though it fades out just as the groove hits its stride.

## The verdict

**_SUN SHERIF_** is a charming, well-made detour in Black Sherif's catalogue. At 7.5/10 it never tries to carry the weight of his studio albums, and it works as a sun-soaked snapshot of an artist taking a breather.

It proves Blacko can write warm, radio-friendly love songs without breaking a sweat, even if the thunder that makes him generational is absent.

SUN SWAGGA shines bright, even when the stakes are low.
