When Gyakie's soprano floated across the continent on her 2020 breakout anthem _Forever_, modern West African music received something rare: a pure vocalist whose emotional intelligence matched her melodic intuition.

Translating that talent into a cohesive debut album is one of the most demanding hurdles in modern pop. As the daughter of highlife icon **Nana Acheampong**, Gyakie was always going to work under a long shadow. Across two EPs (_SEED_ and _My Diary_), the Kumasi singer proved she could craft concise mood pieces that held together.

On her official debut album, **_After Midnight_**, released on August 29, 2025, Gyakie attempts to build an entire universe around the quiet stillness of the witching hour. Across an overstuffed 17 tracks, the project struggles to hold that focus, and genuine flashes end up buried beneath repetitive slow jams, playlist-oriented filler and an unresolved sonic identity.

## The perils of the bloated tracklist

The central flaw of _After Midnight_ is a lack of editorial discipline. Conceiving an album around nocturnal vulnerability is a compelling concept, but stretching that mood across 17 songs requires extraordinary sonic variation.

Too often, the production defaults to interchangeable mid-tempo loops, muted guitar plucks and standard R&B 808s that bleed into one another. When the project tries to shake off this lethargy, it swings wildly in the opposite direction, throwing in UK drill, commercial Amapiano and dancehall cuts that disrupt the delicate late-night ambiance rather than complementing it. Gyakie's voice remains gorgeous throughout, but vocals alone cannot rescue underwritten melodies.

## The tracklist

### 1. intro & 2. fire on the mountain

The album opens with _intro_, a moody Rhodes overture that sets a rainy, contemplative late-night scene. It transitions into _fire on the mountain_, where simmering percussion hints at emotional turbulence. It is a promising opening, and an introspective atmosphere that the rest of the album struggles to sustain.

### 3. damn u (feat. 6LACK)

One of the rare moments where the album achieves true greatness. Teaming up with Atlanta's melancholic R&B crooner **6LACK**, _damn u_ is pure velvet. The two trade verses about an addictive, ill-advised romance with effortless ease. 6LACK's brooding delivery balances Gyakie's airy harmonies, and the track delivers the sophisticated nocturnal soul the album promised.

### 4. harmattan (feat. Shatta Wale)

Pairing Gyakie with dancehall veteran **Shatta Wale** is an ambitious experiment. While Shatta shows surprising restraint over dusty Afro-swing rhythms, the track feels formulaic, functioning more as an industry link-up than a natural fit for Gyakie's sonic palette.

### 5. y2k luv (feat. Omar Sterling)

The undisputed emotional peak of the project. Sampling her father, highlife pioneer **Nana Acheampong**, _y2k luv_ is a gorgeous generational bridge. Gyakie revives golden-era highlife sentimentality while **Omar Sterling** delivers a poised, charismatic verse. It is a heartfelt highlight that proves how potent her music is when rooted in heritage.

### 6. sankofa & 7. after midnight (interlude)

_sankofa_ leans into traditional Akan concepts of spiritual reclamation, offering a touching moment of reflection. It leads into the title interlude, which signals the start of the album's sluggish mid-section.

### 8. i'm not taken (feat. Headie One) & 9. no one

The momentum stalls on _i'm not taken_. UK drill heavyweight **Headie One** delivers a paint-by-numbers guest verse that sounds awkwardly retrofitted onto the track. Follow-up ballad _no one_ meanders over generic acoustic strums, failing to produce a memorable chorus or emotional hook.

### 10. breaking news & 11. house party (feat. Young Jonn)

Attempting to escape the mid-tempo slump, _breaking news_ introduces brass flourishes, leading into _house party_ with Nigerian producer-singer **Young Jonn**. Driven by standard Amapiano log drums, the song sounds aggressively engineered for algorithmic party playlists, jarringly out of place within an album titled _After Midnight_.

### 12. unconditional & 13. story

_unconditional_ offers a pleasant, commercial Afro-pop bounce, but it is immediately followed by _story_, a slow acoustic confession that drags the pacing back down. The constant whiplash between club-ready bangers and drowsy bedroom ballads is where the album loses its direction.

### 14. want it & 15. party galore (feat. Kojey Radical)

_want it_ sinks into forgettable slow-jam filler. _party galore_ benefits from the baritone charisma of British-Ghanaian wordsmith **Kojey Radical**, though the underlying production feels surprisingly thin and repetitive.

### 16. is it worth it? & 17. hallelujah

The final stretch asks the hard questions about ambition and fatigue on _is it worth it?_, before closing with the gospel-tinted _hallelujah_. Gyakie's vocal sincerity on the finale is touching, but the emotional release arrives too late to redeem the preceding stretch of filler.

## The verdict

**_After Midnight_** is a frustrating debut. Gyakie remains one of Ghana's most naturally gifted vocalists, and when she connects, such as on _y2k luv_ and _damn u_, the results are excellent.

At 6/10, the record is weighed down by an unedited 17-track tracklist and an identity crisis that pulls her between intimate highlife-R&B and generic streaming fodder. Trimmed down to a concise, focused 9-track project, this could have been a classic. As it stands, it is a decent but bloated effort that leaves her definitive masterpiece still waiting to be made.
